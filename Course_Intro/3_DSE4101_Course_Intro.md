**What Is a Capstone Project?**


 - A capstone project is a culminating academic experience where stu
dents apply their knowledge to real-world problems or challenges.


 - It encourages integration of skills and knowledge acquired throughout

the program.


 - Provides an opportunity to showcase creativity, critical thinking, and

problem-solving abilities.


 - Students often select a project based on their individual learning pro
files and interests.


 - Typically involves independent identification of topics or problems to

work on.



3


**Our Directed Capstone Projects**


 - Students will select one of three domains:


   - Digital Currency,


   - FinTech, or


   - Digital Economy.


 - Each domain offers two predefined topics provided by the course
coordinators. **The list of topics, along with brief introductions**
**and references, is available on Canvas.**


   - Students may propose a new topic, subject to instructor approval.


 - This structured approach aligns with course objectives and enables

coordinators to offer targeted guidance and support.


 - It fosters focused exploration and promotes a deeper understanding

within the chosen domain.

# **Course Policies**


# **Grading Components**

**Group Components (60%):**


   **Proposals (10%):** Two (2) group project proposals together
account for 10% of the course grade, with 5% allocated to each.


   - **Presentations (25%):** Mid-term and final presentations contribute
10% and 15% to the final grade, respectively. Presentations should

articulate the group’s learning and findings with an engaging and

insightful delivery.


   - **Final Report (25%):** Provide a detailed summary of the group’s
findings and applications of theories and concepts studied.


   - **Group-component grades can be adjusted if there is**

**documented evidence that a member has not contributed**

**adequately.**



9


# **Grading Components Cont’d**

**Individual Components (40%):**


   - **Discussant Duties (10%):** As discussants, students are expected
to engage actively during both the mid-term and final presentation

sessions by asking thoughtful questions, offering constructive

feedback, and responding professionally during peer discussions.


   **Review Reports (30%):**


    - **On a Mid-term Presentation (10%):** The report should evaluate
and critique another group’s presentation for clarity and insights.

    - **On a Final Report (20%):** The report should summarize the key
ideas and findings of the project and offer a thoughtful critique of its

research design, data usage, methodology, and conclusions. Students

are expected to assess both the technical execution and the clarity of

communication.
